CONTENT RIGHTS (App Store Connect > App Information > Content Rights)

THE QUESTION (App Store Connect Help, "App information", read 2026-10-11): "Apps that contain, show, or access third-party content must have all the necessary rights to that content or be otherwise permitted to use it under the laws of each App Store country or region in which they're available." App Store Connect asks: does your app contain, show or access third-party content? If yes, do you have the necessary rights or is your use otherwise permitted?

THE HONEST ANSWER (recommended)
1. "Does your app contain, show, or access third-party content?"  ->  YES.
   Reason: the app does not ship any, but it plays and displays media from third-party servers chosen by the user (live channels, movies, series, logos, posters, program guides). Answering No would describe a bundled-content test; Apple's wording includes "access". An app that answers No and is then seen playing television invites a misrepresentation finding (2.3.1 (b): "if you're dishonest, we don't want to do business with you").
2. "Do you have the necessary rights, or is your use otherwise permitted?"  ->  YES, with this basis (write it in the review notes too; review-notes.txt already does):
   a. IPTVMac is a general-purpose media player. It includes, hosts, sells or recommends no content, and it has no catalogue, no default source and no provider list.
   b. The user supplies the source (an M3U link or an Xtream Codes login from a provider of their choice) and the user is told to use only sources they are authorized to access (the website, the App Store description and the support page say so).
   c. The developer has no relationship with any provider and receives nothing from the app.
   d. Everything the app does ship is licensed: the code (GPL-3.0, owner's own), libmpv and FFmpeg (LGPL builds, see README.md "bundled"), GRDB.swift (MIT), the icon (drawn by scripts/icon/icon.swift, owner's), the fonts of the website (OFL, not in the app), and the screenshots (invented titles, gradient posters, and a frame of "Sintel" by the Blender Foundation under CC BY 3.0).
   e. The review playlist (docs/review/ReviewPlaylist.m3u) points only to Blender Foundation open movies under Creative Commons Attribution, plus their public hosts.
   This is the position of a neutral player (as a browser or any media player stands towards the sites a user opens). It is not a promise that a user's own source is legal: that is between the user and the provider.

WHAT APPLE MAY ASK FOR (guidelines read 2026-10-11)
- 5.2.1: "Make sure your app only includes content that you created or that you have a license to use." -> list in 2d, with the licence texts: LICENSE (GPL-3.0), the LGPL/MIT notices of the bundled libraries, the CC BY 3.0 page https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/ and https://durian.blender.org/sharing/ (Sintel), https://peach.blender.org/about/ (Big Buck Bunny), https://mango.blender.org/sharing/ (Tears of Steel), https://orange.blender.org/ (Elephants Dream).
- 5.2.2: "If your app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request." -> the app uses no named third-party service. The only services it contacts are the user's own. The review playlist hosts (download.blender.org, archive.org, Wikimedia Commons, test-streams.mux.dev, demo.unified-streaming.com) serve openly licensed or demo files publicly; if Apple objects to one host, swap that URL in docs/review/ReviewPlaylist.m3u (no new build needed) and run appstore/check-review-playlist.py --online.
- 5.2.3: "Apps should not facilitate illegal file sharing or include the ability to save, convert, or download media from third-party sources (e.g. Apple Music, YouTube, SoundCloud, Vimeo, etc.) without explicit authorization from those sources." -> THIS IS THE ONE TO WATCH: the Downloads feature (Sources/IPTVMac/Downloads.swift). Answer: it saves, into a folder the user picks, the exact stream that the user's own provider serves to that user, nothing from public video sites, and nothing is converted. If a reviewer still objects, the safest fix is to ship the App Store build without Downloads (a build flag in the other branch), not to argue. This is an owner decision: see CHECKLIST.md.
- Documentation to keep ready: this file, review-notes.txt (second part), the repository link, the privacy policy and support pages, the CC licence pages above.

Do not tick No to make the question go away: see point 1.
